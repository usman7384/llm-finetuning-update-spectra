\begin{abstract}
Studies of what fine-tuning does to a language model often summarise the
weight update $\dW$ by its rank. We compare the rank measures used for this
against the same measures on random matrices of the same shape. On our SFT,
Wikipedia, code and DPO fine-tunes of two small models, a threshold count of
singular values puts the update only 2--5\% below noise, and the entropy
effective rank and the 90\% energy rank put it 12--35\% below; stable rank
puts the same updates 78--94\% below noise. The updates have a few strong
directions on top of a long tail, and the gap to noise grows, then levels
off, with how much weight a measure gives to the top.
The pattern holds for thirteen released base/instruct, post-training-stage
and base/specialist pairs up to 8B parameters. It is not a mark of alignment
training: with the amount of training held fixed, instruction data sits
inside the range of Wikipedia and Python code, DPO gives the same shape and
directions whether its preference labels are real or randomly swapped, and
scrambled text gives the most concentrated update of all. Applying the
baselines to a recent claim that post-training is superficial because the
effective rank of the weights barely changes, we find that a random update
of the same size leaves it almost unchanged too, while cutting the model's
GSM8K accuracy from 60\% to 5\%, and that the edit built on that effective
rank has no measurable effect on GSM8K or on its own benchmark, GPQA. Rank
measures of weight updates should be reported against a noise baseline.
\end{abstract}
