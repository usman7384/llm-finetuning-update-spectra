\begin{abstract}
Studies of what fine-tuning does to a language model often summarise the
weight update $\dW$ by its rank. We compare the rank measures used for this
against the same measures on random matrices of the same shape. On our
fine-tunes of two small models, a threshold count of singular values puts the
update within 2--5\% of noise, and the entropy effective rank and the 90\%
energy rank put it 12--36\% below; stable rank puts the same updates 78--94\%
below noise. The updates have a few strong directions on top of a long tail,
and the gap to noise grows with how much weight a measure gives to the top.
The pattern holds for twelve released base/instruct, preference-stage and
base/specialist pairs up to 8B parameters. It is not a mark of instruction
tuning: with training tokens held fixed, instruction data, Wikipedia and
Python code give updates of similar shape, and scrambled text gives the most
concentrated one. Applying the baselines to a recent claim that post-training
is superficial because the effective rank of the weights barely changes, we
find that a random update of the same size leaves it almost unchanged too.
Rank measures of weight updates should be reported against a noise baseline.
\end{abstract}
