\begin{abstract}
Studies of what fine-tuning does to a language model often summarise the
weight update $\dW$ by its rank. We show that the rank measures most used for
this, a threshold count of singular values, the number of directions holding
90\% of the energy, and the entropy effective rank, give almost the same
values for real updates as for random matrices of the same shape. Read on
their own, they make fine-tuning look like noise. Stable rank, compared
against the same noise baseline, shows that the updates are 1.6--9$\times$
more concentrated than noise: a few strong directions on top of a long tail.
This holds for SFT, DPO and token-matched non-alignment fine-tunes of two
small models, and for seven released base/instruct pairs up to 8B parameters.
The concentration is a property of fine-tuning in general, not of alignment:
fine-tuning on Wikipedia or Python code looks the same. Applying the same
baselines to a recent claim that post-training is superficial because the
effective rank of the weights barely changes, we find that a random update of
the same size leaves it just as unchanged. Rank measures of weight updates
should be reported against a noise baseline.
\end{abstract}
