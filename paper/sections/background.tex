\section{Measuring the shape of an update}
\label{sec:background}

Let $\dW \in \R^{m\times n}$ be the change in one weight matrix,
$r = \min(m,n)$, and $\sigma_1 \geq \dots \geq \sigma_r \geq 0$ its singular
values. We report every measure as a fraction of $r$, so that matrices of
different shapes can be averaged.

\paragraph{Threshold count.} The number of singular values above a fixed
fraction of the largest,
\begin{equation}
  \thresholdrank(\dW) = \frac{1}{r}\,\bigl|\{\, i : \sigma_i \geq 0.01\,\sigma_1 \,\}\bigr| .
\end{equation}
It says nothing about how energy is spread among the values it counts, and a
dense matrix with a slowly decaying spectrum is counted as nearly full rank.

\paragraph{Energy rank.} The number of directions needed to hold a fixed share
of the squared Frobenius norm,
\begin{equation}
  \energyrank(\dW) = \frac{1}{r}\,\min\Bigl\{ k : \textstyle\sum_{i\leq k}\sigma_i^2 \geq 0.9 \sum_{i}\sigma_i^2 \Bigr\} ,
\end{equation}
the measure behind ``rank needed to explain 90\% of the variance'' in
\citet{biderman2024lora}.

\paragraph{Entropy effective rank.} With $p_i = \sigma_i^\gamma / \sum_j \sigma_j^\gamma$,
\begin{equation}
  \entropyrank(\dW) = \frac{1}{r}\exp\Bigl(-\textstyle\sum_i p_i \log p_i\Bigr)
\end{equation}
\citep{roy2007effective}. \citet{wu2025timber} use $\gamma = 1$, and so do we.

\paragraph{Stable rank.}
\begin{equation}
  \stablerank(\dW) = \frac{1}{r}\,\frac{\|\dW\|_F^2}{\|\dW\|_2^2} = \frac{1}{r}\sum_i \frac{\sigma_i^2}{\sigma_1^2}
\end{equation}
\citep{rudelson2007sampling}. It is small when a few directions carry most of
the energy, and unlike the threshold count it is barely affected by many tiny
singular values. We also report the share of energy in the top $k$ singular
values, $\sum_{i\leq k}\sigma_i^2 / \sum_i \sigma_i^2$.

All four measures are invariant to the scale of $\dW$, which matters here:
the updates we compare differ in Frobenius norm by more than three orders of
magnitude.

\paragraph{Noise baselines.} A measure only says something about structure
relative to what an unstructured matrix of the same shape would give. We use
three baselines. (i) The difference between two independent random
initializations of the same architecture, which is what a delta with no
learned structure looks like. (ii) A Gaussian matrix of the same shape as
each $\dW$, used for released models; for the shapes of Qwen2.5-0.5B it gives
a stable rank of 0.434 against 0.436 for the random-init pair, so the two are
interchangeable. (iii) For measures of the whole weight matrix $W$ rather than
the update, a random perturbation with the same Frobenius norm as the real
one, $W_{\text{before}} + G\,\|\dW\|_F/\|G\|_F$ with $G$ Gaussian.

\paragraph{Why (iii) matters.} By Weyl's inequality
\citep{horn2012matrix}, each singular value of $W + \dW$ is within
$\|\dW\|_2$ of the corresponding singular value of $W$. When the update is
small compared with the weights, any function of the spectrum of $W$,
including its effective rank, can only move a little, whatever the update
looks like. An unchanged effective rank of $W$ therefore cannot by itself
distinguish one small update from another; it needs the same-norm comparison.
