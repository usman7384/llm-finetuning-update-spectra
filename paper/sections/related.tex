\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Noise baselines for spectra.} Comparing a spectrum against what
noise would give is old. Parallel analysis \citep{horn1965parallel} keeps only
the factors whose eigenvalues beat those of random data, and
\citet{gavish2014optimal} derive the optimal threshold for separating signal
singular values from Gaussian noise. Random matrix theory has also been used to
study trained weight matrices: their spectra depart from those of random
matrices in heavy-tailed ways that track training quality
\citep{martin2021implicit}, while much of the bulk still follows random-matrix
statistics \citep{thamm2022random}. Our point is
that the recent literature on fine-tuning updates measures rank without such a
baseline, and that this changes what the numbers seem to say.

\paragraph{Superficial alignment.} \citet{zhou2023lima} proposed that
alignment mostly selects a format the pretrained model already supports, and
\citet{lin2024urial} found that base and aligned models differ mainly on
stylistic tokens. \citet{wu2025timber} looked for weight-level evidence through
the effective rank of released models (\S\ref{sec:timber}).
\citet{chaudhury2025alignment} locate the effect of preference tuning in a few
middle layers by activation patching, a question about activations that our
weight analysis does not address.

\paragraph{The rank of fine-tuning updates.} Fine-tuning can be carried out in
a small random subspace \citep{aghajanyan2021intrinsic}, the idea behind LoRA
\citep{hu2022lora}, and fine-tuned models can be stored as low-rank
approximations of the update \citep{ryu2023efficient}. Measured directly, full
fine-tuning updates need a rank 10--100 times larger than typical LoRA ranks to
hold 90\% of their energy \citep{biderman2024lora}, and reinforcement-learning
updates are reported as nearly full rank, 99.2--99.8\% of the maximum
\citep{mukherjee2025rl}. Both statements count directions; our results agree
with them and add that the same updates are far more concentrated than noise at
the top of the spectrum. \citet{shuttleworth2025lora} compare singular vectors
of fine-tuned and pretrained $W$ and find that LoRA introduces new dominant
directions that full fine-tuning does not. Delta-CoMe \citep{ping2024deltacome}
compresses updates by giving more bits to the top singular vectors and finds
that plain low-rank truncation hurts, consistent with our finding that the tail
matters. \citet{paul2026spectral} use stable rank among other features of LoRA
updates to tell training objectives apart.

\paragraph{Low precision.} Rounding weights to bf16 cancels small updates
\citep{zamirai2020bfloat16}, and in reinforcement learning this can make
updates look sparse \citep{zhu2025path}. We found the same effect in our own
first runs (\S\ref{sec:setup}) and show that it also inflates the threshold
count of released checkpoints (\S\ref{sec:released}). A likely reason for the
concentration itself is that gradients of linear layers are approximately low
rank \citep{zhao2024galore}, so a sum of gradient steps inherits a few
dominant directions.
