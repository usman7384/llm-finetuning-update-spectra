\section{Behavioural results from the same runs}
\label{app:behavior}

The spectral results do not depend on any evaluation, but the Qwen runs were
also evaluated. We report the results here as context; all are single-seed.

\begin{table}[h]

\label{tab:evals}

\paragraph{SFT trades capability for style.} SFT gains about three points of
instruction following and loses 4.7 points of GSM8K, 2.6 standard errors of
the difference (Table~\ref{tab:evals}). Refusals rise by the same amount on
safe and unsafe XSTest prompts, which looks like a formatting effect rather
than learned refusal; Alpaca contains almost no refusals.

\paragraph{A rank-1 adapter recovers the style gain.} In a LoRA sweep
\citep{hu2022lora} over ranks 1--128 on the same data and token budget, a
rank-1 adapter recovers 137\% of SFT's IFEval gain, consistent with the
observation that low-rank LoRA can match full fine-tuning on small
post-training datasets \citep{thinkingmachines2025lora}. GSM8K falls with
rank, from 32.4\% at rank 1 to 17.7\% at rank 128, as validation loss rises
(Table~\ref{tab:lora}). We do not lean on that trend: the lowest learning rate
tried won at every rank, so the grid was probably too high.

\paragraph{The adapter points where full fine-tuning points.} Comparing the
top-$r$ left singular vectors of the full SFT update with the column space of
each rank-$r$ adapter's $B$ matrix, the mean cosine of the principal angles is
11.3$\times$ what random subspaces give at rank 1, 2.7$\times$ at rank 8 and
1.1$\times$ at rank 128 (Table~\ref{tab:lora}); the ratio falls because random
subspaces overlap more as they grow. This concerns overlap with the dominant
directions of $\dW$ and is compatible with \citet{shuttleworth2025lora}, who
find that LoRA adds new singular directions to $W$ that full fine-tuning does
not.

\paragraph{DPO is a smaller dose.} DPO on top of SFT barely moves the
evaluations (IFEval $+0.2$, GSM8K $-0.5$, ARC $-0.3$). Its update has the same
average stable rank as SFT's at about a fifteenth of the size, though the two
spread it differently across matrix types (Table~\ref{tab:pertype}).
