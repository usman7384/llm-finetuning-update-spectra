\section{What the updates do behaviourally}
\label{sec:behavior}

The spectral results do not depend on any evaluation, but the same Qwen runs
were evaluated, and the results give some context for what a concentrated
update does. We report them briefly; all are single-seed.

\paragraph{SFT trades capability for style.} SFT gains about three points of
instruction following and loses 4.7 points of GSM8K, about 3.6 standard
errors (Table~\ref{tab:evals}). Refusals rise by the same amount on safe and
unsafe XSTest prompts, which looks like a formatting effect rather than
learned refusal; Alpaca contains almost no refusals.

\paragraph{A rank-1 adapter recovers the style gain.} In a LoRA sweep over
ranks 1--128 on the same data and token budget, a rank-1 adapter recovers
137\% of SFT's IFEval gain, in line with reports that small ranks suffice for
post-training-style workloads \citep{thinkingmachines2025lora}, and GSM8K
falls steadily with rank, from 32.4\% at
rank 1 to 17.7\% at rank 128, alongside rising validation loss (1.38 to 1.61).
We do not lean on the GSM8K trend: the lowest learning rate tried
($10^{-4}$) won at every rank, so the grid was probably too high
(Appendix~\ref{app:lora}).

\paragraph{The adapter points where full fine-tuning points.} Comparing the
top-$r$ left singular vectors of the full SFT update with the column space of
each rank-$r$ adapter's $B$ matrix, the mean cosine of the principal angles is
0.322 at rank 1 against 0.028 for random subspaces (11$\times$), 0.287 against
0.105 at rank 8 (2.7$\times$) and 0.526 against 0.465 at rank 128. The ratio
falls with rank because random subspaces overlap more as they grow. This is
about overlap with the dominant directions of $\dW$, and is compatible with
\citet{shuttleworth2025lora}, who find that LoRA introduces new singular
directions into $W$ that full fine-tuning does not.

\paragraph{DPO is a smaller dose of the same shape.} DPO on top of SFT barely
changes the evaluations (IFEval $+0.2$, GSM8K $-0.5$, ARC $-0.3$). Its update
has the same average stable rank as SFT's (0.144 against 0.145) at about one
fifteenth of the size, although the two distribute it differently across
matrix types (Appendix~\ref{app:pertype}).
