\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

When a language model is fine-tuned, each weight matrix $W$ changes by an
update $\dW$. A natural way to ask what fine-tuning did is to ask how many
independent directions $\dW$ uses, that is, its rank. A low-rank update
suggests a small, targeted change; a full-rank one suggests a diffuse change.

The question matters most for alignment. The superficial alignment hypothesis
\citep{zhou2023lima} holds that alignment mostly teaches a model which style
to answer in, and output-level evidence supports it \citep{lin2024urial}. If
that is true, one might expect alignment to leave a small, structured
footprint in the weights, and recent work has looked for one in the rank of
$\dW$ and of $W$ \citep{wu2025timber}. Related work reports that fine-tuning
updates are ``high rank'' \citep{biderman2024lora} or ``nearly full-rank''
\citep{mukherjee2025rl}.

Real updates are full rank in the strict sense, so these readings rest on
softer measures: the numerical rank, the number of directions that hold 90\%
of the energy \citep{biderman2024lora}, or the entropy effective rank
\citep{roy2007effective, wu2025timber}. \citet{biderman2024lora} also compared
against noise: the spectrum of a fully fine-tuned weight matrix looks like that
of the pretrained one plus low-magnitude Gaussian noise, and they concluded
that nothing about it is unusual. We began this project with the same
impression. Counting the singular values of an SFT update above 1\% of the
largest gave 97\% of full rank, and the difference between two random
initialisations of the same model, which is pure noise, gave 99.6\%. By that
measure, fine-tuning looks almost like noise.

It is not. Stable rank \citep{rudelson2007sampling}, computed from the same
singular values, puts the update 4.5 times below noise, and its top direction
holds 6.6 times as much of the energy (4.2\% against 0.6\%). The two answers
differ because the
measures weigh different parts of the spectrum, and because measures of $W$
cannot see much of $\dW$ at all. Comparing against noise is an old idea in
statistics \citep{horn1965parallel, gavish2014optimal} and has been used to
study trained weights \citep{martin2021implicit, thamm2022random}; this paper
applies it to the update itself, measure by measure.

Our findings:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Counting measures compress the gap to noise.} On our SFT,
  Wikipedia, code and DPO fine-tunes, the threshold count is only 2--5\%
  below a random matrix of the same shape, and eRank and the 90\% energy rank
  are 12--35\% below it; stable rank is 78--94\% below. Along the whole family
  of R\'enyi ranks, the gap grows with how much weight a measure gives to the
  top directions, then levels off (\S\ref{sec:metrics}).
  \item \textbf{Updates have a few strong directions and a long tail}, and on
  average are more concentrated than the pretrained weights they are added to
  (\S\ref{sec:headtail}).
  \item \textbf{The shape does not single out alignment training.} With the
  amount of training held fixed, the data does shape an update (code is the
  least concentrated, the more so with more training), but instruction SFT
  sits inside the range of Wikipedia and Python code. DPO gives the same shape,
  and the same directions, whether it learns real preferences or randomly
  swapped ones that teach nothing. Text with its word order scrambled gives
  the most concentrated update of all (\S\ref{sec:controls}).
  \item \textbf{It holds for released models}, thirteen base/instruct,
  post-training-stage and base/specialist pairs up to 8B parameters. On three
  bf16 updates (Qwen2.5-1.5B and the OLMo 2 DPO and RL stages), much of what
  the threshold count counts may be rounding (\S\ref{sec:released}).
  \item \textbf{It changes how earlier evidence reads.} An unchanged effective
  rank of $W$ after post-training, read by \citet{wu2025timber} as a sign that
  post-training is superficial, is what a random update of the same size also
  produces, even one that cuts the model's GSM8K accuracy from 60\% to 5\%.
  The edit they build on that effective rank has no measurable effect on
  GSM8K or on their own benchmark, GPQA-Diamond (\S\ref{sec:timber}).
\end{enumerate}

The practical message is short: report rank measures of weight updates next to
the same measure on noise, check that low-precision training or storage did
not erase the update, and test claims about alignment against fine-tuning
without alignment content.
