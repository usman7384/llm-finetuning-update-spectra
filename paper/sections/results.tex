Figure~\ref{fig:metrics} and Table~\ref{tab:metrics} show the four measures on
our updates. By the threshold count, every Qwen update is at 99\% of full rank,
like the noise. The energy rank and the effective rank are lower than noise,
but not by much. Stable rank tells a different story: every update is several
times more concentrated than noise.

Table~\ref{tab:gaps} puts a number on this. The three counting measures put
the updates 0.4--31\% below noise; stable rank puts them 65--87\% below, that
is, 2.8--7.9 times lower. Every interval excludes zero, so all four measures
do detect that the updates are not random. The problem is size. A reader who
sees ``99\% of full rank'' or ``89\% effective rank'' without the noise value
next to it will conclude that the update is diffuse and unstructured, which is
what we concluded at first. The pattern holds for every matrix type
(Appendix~\ref{app:pertype}).

\subsection{A few strong directions and a long tail}
\label{sec:headtail}

\begin{figure}[t]
\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/spectrum_head_tail.pdf}
\end{center}
\caption{Where the energy sits, averaged over the 168 matrices of
Qwen2.5-0.5B. Left: share of energy in the top fraction of singular
directions. Right: singular values relative to the largest. The updates hold
far more energy in their top directions than noise does, then decay slowly
over a long tail, much like the pretrained weights (dashed).}
\label{fig:spectrum}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:spectrum} shows why the measures disagree. At the top of the
spectrum the updates are far more concentrated than noise: the top singular
value of the Qwen SFT update holds 5.1\% of its energy against 0.6\% for noise,
and the top 8 directions hold 12.0\% against 4.9\%. Below that the
spectrum decays slowly, and that long tail still holds most of the energy.
Stable rank is dominated by the top, so it sees the concentration. The
counting measures count directions, most of which are in the tail, so they see
a nearly full-rank matrix.

Both descriptions are therefore true of the same matrices: fine-tuning
updates are high rank \citep{biderman2024lora} and they are concentrated. The
tail is not noise to be discarded either; removing it hurts the model
(\S\ref{sec:cutoff}).

The pretrained weights have a very similar shape (dashed line). Their stable
rank is 0.178 for Qwen2.5-0.5B and 0.140 for SmolLM2-360M
(Table~\ref{tab:metrics}). So the updates are concentrated compared with
noise, and at least as concentrated as the weights they are added to:
1.2--1.6$\times$ more on Qwen and 1.6--3.1$\times$ more on SmolLM2. Being
concentrated is normal for trained matrices; the point is that counting
measures hide it.

\subsection{It is about fine-tuning, not alignment}
\label{sec:controls}

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{table}

A concentrated SFT update says nothing about alignment unless fine-tuning on
other data looks different. It does not. On both models, the updates from
fine-tuning on Wikipedia text and on Python code are as concentrated as the
SFT update (Table~\ref{tab:metrics}), even though they contain no
instructions, chat format or preferences. The DPO update has the same average
stable rank as SFT (0.144 against 0.145) at a fifteenth of the size.

Size and concentration are separate. On Qwen the updates differ eighteen-fold
in size (mean Frobenius norm from 0.019 for DPO to 0.349 for wikitext), while
their stable rank stays between 0.110 and 0.154.

The differences between tasks are real but small (Table~\ref{tab:paired}).
Repeating a run with a new seed and data sample moves stable rank by at most
0.003. Wikitext is more concentrated than code on both models, by the same
0.044. SFT falls between the two controls, but not at a consistent place: on
Qwen it cannot be told apart from code, on SmolLM2 it is close to wikitext.
What we can say is that the alignment update lies inside the range of the two
non-alignment updates.

Our fine-tunes are small. Released instruct models go through much larger and
more varied post-training, so we measured seven official pairs directly
(Figure~\ref{fig:released}, Table~\ref{tab:released}). The pattern is the
same. The threshold count reads 96--99\% for every update, close to noise;
stable rank puts every update 1.6--9.1$\times$ below noise, including
Qwen3-8B at 4.5$\times$. Qwen2.5-0.5B is the least concentrated, and less so
than our own SFT of the same base (0.280 against 0.145); we do not know why.
